Notes on the data

  • Wages use the ABS Wage Price Index (total hourly rates of pay excluding bonuses, all sectors, seasonally adjusted). It holds the mix of jobs constant, so it measures pay rates rather than changes in who is employed.
  • Inflation is the ABS all groups CPI for Australia. The ABS re-referenced it to September 2025 = 100 when the complete monthly CPI was introduced; this does not affect growth rates.
  • Money supply is broad money (M3 plus other borrowings from the private sector by non-ADI financial institutions). It is built from the RBA's monthly growth rates, which are seasonally adjusted and adjusted for breaks in the series. The RBA's published dollar levels are not break-adjusted (for example, the July 2019 change in data collection), so growth calculated from them understates the true increase.
  • House prices are the BIS residential property price index for Australia. It is a spliced series: to the December quarter 2021 it is the ABS index for all dwellings in the eight capital cities; from the March quarter 2022, after the ABS discontinued that index, it covers all dwellings across the whole country. Compare movements either side of 2022 with care. It is usually published a quarter or two behind the other series.
  • Real wages are wages divided by CPI: above 100 means pay rates have grown faster than consumer prices since 1997.
  • RBA Table D1 publication date: 30 Oct 2026.
